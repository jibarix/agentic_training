# AI Agents for Social Science and Society (University of Chicago / KnowledgeLab, Winter 2026)

- **URL:** https://github.com/KnowledgeLab/AI-Agents-for-Social-Science-and-Society-2026 (README serves as the syllabus); notebooks at https://github.com/KnowledgeLab/AI-Agents-for-Social-Science-and-Society-2026/tree/main/Tutorials-Homework_Notebooks
- **Instructor:** James A. Evans. TAs: Jesse Zhou, Shiyang Lai, Avi Oberoi, Gio Choi.
- **Level/prereqs:** Python familiarity required. Week 0 review covers the Python tutorial and *Deep Learning* chapters 2-3 (linear algebra, probability). Pitched at graduate students and advanced undergraduates in computational social science (inferred from content).
- **Format:** In person, Fridays 1:30-4:20pm, 1155 E. 59th St., room 295. Weekly Jupyter tutorial and homework notebooks (released Tuesday, due Wednesday noon) run locally, on UChicago RCC, or in Colab (Pro recommended). Weekly 300-500-word reading memos with peer upvoting, 5-minute Ignite-style code presentations, and recorded TA labs. An Anthropic/AWS grant gives students Claude Code credits.
- **Grading:** reading, memos, and presentations 30%; weekly exercises 35% (top 6 of 0-10 grades); final project 35%.
- **Public materials:** syllabus README with full reading lists (citations only, no URLs) and 9 Jupyter notebooks (weeks 1-9) plus a `data/` folder. No slides or videos are public; lab recordings and the Claude Code setup tutorial are on Canvas.
- **License:** none stated.

The course treats LLM-based agents as tools for social science: research assistants, simulated subjects and "digital doubles," advisors, and automated scientists. It builds up from deep-learning foundations (feed-forward nets, embeddings, transformers, diffusion), then moves to multi-agent social simulation, experimental and causal designs that mix AI and human subjects, fine-tuning and benchmarking, interpretability and steering, RL, multimodal and embodied agents, and alignment and ethics. Each week's readings are organized into "AI Foundations/Techniques," "AI/Social Perspectives," and "Social Designs" strands, and many of the papers come from Evans's lab. The final project must combine at least three data types and three model types from three different weeks, validate its results qualitatively, and be published as a long public blog post with open code.
