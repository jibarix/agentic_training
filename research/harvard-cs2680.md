# CS 2680 — Modern AI Systems: Agents and System Optimizations (Harvard University, SEAS, Fall 2026)

- **URLs:** course site https://cs2680.seas.harvard.edu/ (also https://cs2680.com) ; assignments https://cs2680.seas.harvard.edu/assignments/index.html ; final project https://cs2680.seas.harvard.edu/project.html ; policy https://cs2680.seas.harvard.edu/policy.html ; starter code as archives on the assignment pages (the former starter repo https://github.com/HarvardMadSys/cs2680_assignments returned 404 on 2026-10-18) ; student blog https://blog.cs2680.com/
- **Instructor:** Juncheng Yang (Harvard MaDSys, the Measurement and Design of Systems group). TFs: Yiyu Liu, Advaith Ravishankar, Charles Wang, Yunjia Zheng.
- **Level/prereqs:** Graduate computer-systems course. CS61 and at least one graduate-level systems course; comfort with Python and PyTorch. The policy page lists the systems vocabulary it assumes (tail latency, concurrency, virtual memory, caching and so on) and says the course is a poor fit if most of it is new.
- **Format:** Two meetings a week (Monday/Wednesday), 25 meetings from Sep 2 to Dec 7. Part I (to Oct 14) covers using and designing agents. Part II (from Oct 19) covers LLM serving and agentic systems. Grading: five individual assignments (10/10/20/20/10%), a final project (20%), participation (10%); no exam. Two student-led paper discussions (Oct 14, Nov 16) and three assignment-sharing sessions (a fourth, on Dec 2, was dropped by 2026-11-11). AI use is encouraged and must be disclosed; every assignment hands in the Claude Code session logs.
- **Public materials (checked 2026-11-11):** slide decks for the eight instructor lectures of Part I and three of Part II (Oct 19; Oct 21, whose second part was given on Nov 2; Nov 4), an assignments-feedback deck (Nov 2), plus Assignment 3 and Assignment 4 decks; two more decks (Oct 26 sharing, Oct 28 Assignment 3 feedback) are password-protected; nine annotated reading pages linked from the home page (112 entries, each with background, problem, key idea and why it matters; the Nov 4 page was linked by 2026-11-11); seven more annotated reading pages that load at the same URL pattern but are not linked from the home page (170 entries, covering Sep 30, Oct 7 and five Part II meetings; see Notes); three reference notes (agent evaluation and benchmarks, cost-effective agents, transformers and LLM foundations); full specs for Assignments 1--4 and the final project; starter archives for Assignments 2--4 linked from their handouts (assignment2.tar.gz, assignment3.tar.gz, assignment4.tar.gz); an Assignment 4 engine-conventions page on the leaderboard site; a student blog. No recordings (the policy page says lectures are not recorded).
- **License:** None stated on the site. The three public repositories it had on 2026-10-16 had no license file; the starter repository returned 404 on 2026-10-18.
- **Access limits:** Assignments 2 and 3 call a course model API (api.cs2680.com) with a key issued to enrolled students; auditors get no computing. Assignments 4 and 5 use the course GPU cluster. Outside the course, Assignment 2 can be done only by pointing the harness at your own OpenAI-compatible endpoint, and Assignment 3's official scoring (leaderboard) is not available.

**Overview.** The course follows one agentic system from the agent loop down through the serving stack. Students first use a coding agent (Claude Code), then build their own harness around a model API, measure it and cut its cost, then serve an open-weight model themselves and optimize the layers underneath: batching and scheduling, KV-cache and prefix reuse, routing, quantization and speculative decoding. The five assignments are one system taken apart step by step (watch the agent, build the loop, make every token count, own the serving engine, optimize the full stack). The instructor's framing is that agents are a systems problem: one user prompt triggers many model calls, and the quantities to manage are tokens, turns, latency and dollars per solved task.
